**Short answer: the Quran describes the moon as passing ordained stations — crescent, full, then waning — so people can count months and years.** It gives timekeeping, not an astronomy textbook: light phases you can see, tied to worship and the calendar.

For practice, see [Moon Sighting (Hilal) — How Ramadan, Eid and Hajj Months Begin](/learn/duties/moon-sighting-hilal/), the [Dua on Sighting the New Moon](/duas/ramadan-fasting/new-moon-sighting/), [Hijri Converter](/tools/hijri-converter/) and [Islamic Calendar](/calendar/). ## The 4 key verses

> `He made the sun a radiance and the moon a light, and ordained for it mansions, that you may know the count of years and calculation.` — Quran 10:5

> `And the moon — We ordained for it mansions, till it returns like an old date-stalk. Neither the sun overtakes the moon, nor the night outruns the day — each swims in an orbit.` — Quran 36:39–40

> `They ask you about the crescents. Say: they are timings for people and for Hajj.` — Quran 2:189

> `The count of months with Allah is twelve — four of them sacred.` — Quran 9:36

Plus: `the sun and the moon run by calculation (husban)` (55:5).

## What manazil means

`Manazil al-qamar` — the moon's nightly stations. Each night it appears slightly changed: thin crescent after sunset, waxing semicircle, full bright disc, then waning back to a curved sliver `like an old date-stalk` — dry, bent, pale. Classical tafsir (Ibn Kathir, Jalalayn tradition) reads this as the visible monthly cycle Allah fixed for reckoning.

That is why the Islamic month is **29 or 30 days, never 28 or 31**: the Prophet ﷺ said fast when you see it, break when you see it, complete thirty if clouds hide it (Bukhari/Muslim). Astronomy can predict; sighting starts the month.

## Orbit, not collision

`Each swims in an orbit` (36:40, also 21:33). Sun and moon keep their courses — day and night alternate without one destroying the other's role. The Quran ties this order to gratitude: signs in dead earth revived, `sun and moon in orbit`, ships laden (see Surah Ya-Sin 33–40).

## What Islam does not claim

* **No orbital-mechanics textbook.** The Quran describes observable phases and their purpose — worship times, Hajj, fasting — not kilometres, gravity or landing sites.
* **No 54:1 confusion.** `The Hour drew near and the moon split` (54:1) is a distinct prophetic sign, not a phase of the cycle.
* **No date-setting from phases alone.** Eclipses, supermoons or blood moons do not signal the Hour's date; during an eclipse Muslims pray, give charity and remember Allah (Bukhari/Muslim).

## From crescent to calendar

1. **Crescent (hilal):** month begins on verified sighting — Ramadan, Shawwal, Dhul-Hijjah.
2. **Full (badr):** mid-month brightness — the 13th–15th fasts (ayam al-bid).
3. **Waning:** month closes at 29 or 30; next crescent hunt begins.
4. **Twelve months:** Muharram to Dhul-Hijjah, with Rajab, Dhul-Qadah, Dhul-Hijjah and Muharram sacred (9:36).

Allah knows best — the cycle you see each month is the clock the Quran points to.
